# Link cards

For the end of a note, where the question is what to read next. A card carries a
title, a line of description and an eyebrow naming where it leads, so the reader
chooses between destinations rather than guessing from four words of link text.

<CardGrid>
  <LinkCard
    href="/stash/react/hooks/use-debounce"
    eyebrow="react/hooks"
    title="useDebounce"
    description="Debouncing a rapidly updating value without dropping the last one."
  />
  <LinkCard
    href="/stash/typescript/patterns/result-type"
    eyebrow="typescript/patterns"
    title="Result type"
    description="Error handling without exceptions, and what it costs at the boundary."
  />
</CardGrid>

`<CardGrid>` lays them out in two columns on a wide screen and one on a narrow
one. A single card is fine, and three read as a list of equals. That is the
point, so do not use cards to rank.

## Inside the prose

Cards interrupt reading, so they belong at a stopping point, which is the end of
a section or the end of the note. A reference made in passing stays an
[inline link](/stash/authoring/markdown/links), which keeps the sentence intact.

<CardGrid>
  <LinkCard
    href="/stash/authoring/markdown/links"
    eyebrow="authoring/markdown"
    title="Links"
    description="The four kinds of link, and which of them the build checks."
  />
</CardGrid>
